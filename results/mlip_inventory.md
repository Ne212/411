## 2. Training datasets and their DFT level

The functional of the training data decides what a model "is" and what reference it should be compared
with. All rows were taken from the documentation cited.

| Dataset | Content | DFT level (code) | Energy convention | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MPtrj | 1.58 M bulk relaxation frames from Materials Project (Sep 2022) | PBE / PBE+U (VASP, MP settings) | CHGNet trains on MP2020-corrected energies; MACE-MP trains on raw VASP energies | CHGNet README; MACE README; figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.23713842 |
| sAlex | sub-sampled Alexandria, 10.4 M structures | PBE / PBE+U, MP-compatible | total energies | GRACE docs, SevenNet docs |
| OMat24 | ≈100 M off-equilibrium bulk structures | PBE / PBE+U, VASP 54 PPs (differs from MP's PPs) | total energies; **not directly compatible with MP energies** | fairchem `uma.md`; arXiv:2410.12771 |
| MatPES | PBE and r2SCAN, no +U | PBE / r2SCAN | separate scales | MACE README; arXiv:2503.04070 |
| OC20 | slabs + adsorbates (metals and intermetallics, no oxides) | **RPBE**, VASP 5.4, no dispersion | original OC20 models: adsorption energies; UMA's `oc20`: recomputed total energies | fairchem `uma.md`, `catalysts/models.md`; Chanussot et al., ACS Catal. 11, 6059 (2021), doi:10.1021/acscatal.0c04525 |
| OC22 | oxide surfaces | PBE+U, spin-polarised | total energies | fairchem `uma.md` |
| SMAX | maximum-entropy structures for many elements | not read | — | arXiv:2602.23489 |

**Discrepancy to keep in mind:** the MACE-MH-1 model card lists "OC20 … (PBE)", while FAIRChem documents
OC20 as RPBE. The head name is `oc20_usemppbe`. **Not verified** which functional MH-1's OC20 head was
trained on. The smoke test shows that MACE-MH-1-oc20, SevenNet-Omni-oc20 and DPA-3.1-oc20m all give
E(Au bulk, a = 4.08 Å) = −2.58…−2.59 eV/atom (UMA `oc20`: −2.594), against −3.17…−3.24 eV/atom for every PBE-task model.
So all three OC20 heads share one energy scale, different from the PBE heads. That is consistent with
RPBE, but it does not prove it.

would show up as a factor of 160).

Observations (to be quantified in Stage 2, not conclusions yet):
* For models trained on essentially the same PBE data, the energy of the **isolated Hg atom spans
  −0.61 eV (TensorNet-MatPES) to +0.38 eV (M3GNet-MatPES)**, about 1 eV. The isolated atom has no
  neighbours inside the cutoff, so the model returns only its per-element reference term. That term was
  never trained against a free-atom DFT energy.
* MACE-MPA-0 and MACE-OMAT-0 give bit-identical isolated-atom energies (−0.12454674 eV for Hg), and
  NequIP-OAM-L and Allegro-OAM-L both give −0.1235 eV. This points to fixed per-element reference
  energies (E0 / energy shifts) shared between models. Not yet checked inside the model files.
* `DPA-3.1-3M-AgAu-PBED3` uses a different energy scale (Au bulk −3.88 eV/atom), and its dataset has no
  Hg. Use it only as a flagged diagnostic.

## 5. Miro's ALIGNN-FF script (upstream commit 7d60709, 2026-11-03)

Run unchanged (md5-identical copy, CPU, as in the script) in `work/survey/upstream_runs/alignn_ff/`:
**all four numbers and the final Hg positions are reproduced exactly**: ontop −0.747, bridge −0.954,
"fcc" −1.099, "hcp" −1.055 eV (82 s on the CPU, alignn 2026.11.1, model `alignn_ff_mptrj`,
figshare file 70840341).

Geometry check (`work/survey/check_miro_sites.py`):
* the script's **"fcc" point lies exactly above a 2nd-layer Au atom → it is the hcp hollow** in the ASE
  fcc111 convention;
* the script's **"hcp" start point is not a high-symmetry site** (0.96 Å from atoms of all three
  layers). Hg relaxes towards the hcp hollow and stops 0.11 Å short of it at fmax = 0.05 eV/Å;
* the true fcc hollow is not computed.

Step-by-step bridge to the 411 protocol (`work/survey/protocol_bridge.py`, ALIGNN-FF-mptrj on the GPU,
sites placed with ASE names, i.e. correct fcc/hcp). E_ads in eV:

| Step | ontop | bridge | fcc | hcp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 Miro's settings (3×3×3, a = 4.08, clean slab not relaxed, Hg free, fmax 0.05, 10 Å atom box) | −0.748 | −0.954 | −1.120 | −1.099 |
| S1 + clean slab relaxed | −0.282 | −0.633 | −0.596 | −0.573 |
| S2 + own a0 = 4.1714 Å | −0.340 | −0.659 | −0.660 | −0.631 |
| S3 + 3×3×4, vacuum 12 Å | −0.331 | −0.662 | −0.652 | −0.641 |
| S4 + fmax 0.02, Hg z-only, 15 Å box (= 411 protocol) | −0.332 | −0.663 | −0.653 | −0.642 |

S4 equals the independent `hg_au_protocol.py` run to 1 meV. **Almost the whole difference (0.47–0.52 eV)
comes from not relaxing the clean slab in S0.** At a = 4.08 Å the slab is 2.2 % compressed relative to
ALIGNN's own a0. When Hg adsorbs, the top layer relaxes, and that strain-relief energy is counted as
adsorption energy. ALIGNN-FF-mptrj, 411 protocol: isolated-Hg error vs PBE = +0.44 eV
(E_coh,model 0.611 vs E_coh,PBE 0.169 eV). Scheme D gives ontop +0.110, bridge −0.221, fcc −0.211,
hcp −0.200 eV (PBE 411: ontop −0.266, bridge −0.315, fcc −0.333, hcp −0.333 eV). Also note that ALIGNN
ranks bridge ≥ fcc, unlike PBE.
